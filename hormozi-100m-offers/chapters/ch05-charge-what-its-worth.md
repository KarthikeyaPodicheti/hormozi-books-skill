# Chapter 5: Pricing — Charge What It's Worth

## Core Idea
People buy when perceived VALUE exceeds PRICE (price-to-value discrepancy). Widen the gap by raising value first, then price — never by discounting. Premium pricing is both the smart and the moral choice: it funds better service and increases client investment, which increases their results.

## Frameworks Introduced
- Price-to-Value Discrepancy
- Virtuous Cycle of Price
- Higher Price = Higher Value (literally)

## Key Concepts
- "Charge as high a price as you can say out loud without cracking a smile" (Dan Kennedy). "There is no strategic benefit to being second cheapest."
- The dead-broke pricing process everyone follows: scan market → average → go slightly below → "a little more for less." Your competitors are broke; don't copy them.
- Virtuous cycle of RAISING prices: ↑emotional investment → ↑perceived value → ↑results → attracts best (easiest-to-satisfy) clients → ↑margin to fund quality and scale. Cutting price does the exact opposite.
- Wine study: identical wine rated best when labeled expensive. Price itself creates allure; being dramatically above market makes people assume something categorically different is happening.
- Clients who pay the most pay the most attention — for services requiring client participation, a price that stings improves adherence and outcomes.
- Gym Launch pricing: market had $500/month competitors and one $5k player; Hormozi entered at $16,000 (3x highest) then upsold 35% into $42k/year. Justified because clients averaged +$239k/year revenue in 11 months — buyers still got an enormous deal.
- Conviction requirement: you can only charge big prices when you've outworked self-doubt and know delivery works.

## Anti-patterns
- Pricing at "market rate minus 10%" — pricing for perfect efficiency = permanent subsistence.
- Discounting to close ("getting customers is not the objective; making money is").
- Charging premium without delivering — guarantees on a bad product backfire.

## Worked Example
Hormozi convincing his dad: "If I made you $239,000 extra this year, would you pay me $42,000? Nothing up front." Dad instantly got it — that reframing from cost to ROI is how premium prices get said out loud.

## Key Takeaways
- Local Local service: anchor your base price+ sprint pricing against outcome value (a local service business's new patient is worth thousands), not against freelancer rates.
- Raise value before raising price so every increase still leaves the buyer feeling they won.
- A sting on purchase is a feature: invested clients follow through and get results.
