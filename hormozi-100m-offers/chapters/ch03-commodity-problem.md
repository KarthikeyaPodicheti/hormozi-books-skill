# Chapter 3: Pricing — The Commodity Problem

## Core Idea
Commodities are priced by market efficiency: competition drives price toward cost until owners are slaves to their businesses. The escape is differentiation — becoming incomparable so purchases become value-driven instead of price-driven.

## Frameworks Introduced
- Price-driven vs value-driven purchases
- "Maintenance is a myth" / Grow or Die (must beat ~9%/year market growth)

## Key Concepts
- Commodity test: prospect thinks "these are pretty much the same, I'll buy the cheaper one."
- Three ways to grow (get more customers / increase purchase value / increase frequency); really two buckets (more customers, more value per customer).
- Commoditized marketing all looks identical: "you pay us, we work, maybe results." Reasonable but duplicable.
- A Grand Slam Offer recalibrates the prospect's value-meter — comparison becomes too difficult.

## Anti-patterns
- Slashing price to fill capacity while margins die.
- Staying "competitive" with lookalike providers forever.
- Assuming more traffic will fix a commoditized offer.

## Worked Example
The agency before/after from ch02 restated as the commodity problem: the retainer agency was forced to match cheaper rivals or lose clients; the pay-on-performance + guarantee version had no comparable rival, so price resistance disappeared.

## Key Takeaways
- If your service can be swapped for a cheaper twin, pricing power is zero regardless of quality.
- Fulfillment cost doesn't change between commodity and Grand Slam versions — only packaging does.
- Local Local service check: does a local service business comparing us to a freelancer see "same thing"? If yes, restructure the offer, don't cut price.
