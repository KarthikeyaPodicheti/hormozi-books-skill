# Chapter 14: Enhancing The Offer — Bonuses

## Core Idea
One offer presented as a stack of named bonuses is worth more than the same offer whole. Anchor price on the core offer, then stack bonuses until stated value dwarfs price and the "rubber band in their mind snaps."

## Frameworks Introduced
- Bonus Bullets (11 rules)
- Bonuses with scarcity / bonuses with urgency
- Other people's products as bonuses (+ negotiated commissions)

## Key Concepts
- Never discount the core offer to close — add a matched bonus. Discounting teaches customers prices are negotiable; bonuses put you in strength and goodwill.
- 1-on-1 sequence: ask for the sale FIRST; if yes, reveal bonuses post-signature (reinforces decision); if no, present the bonus matching their exact obstacle and ask again ("fair enough?"). Reciprocity makes repeated asks land.
- Bonus bullets: (1) always include them (2) benefit-driven names (3) tell them how it relates / what it is / its origin story / how it improves life faster-easier (4) proof (5) paint life-after-use vividly (6) attach and justify a price tag (7) tools & checklists > trainings (8) each bonus must kill one specific objection or pre-solve their NEXT problem (9) bonus value should EXCEED core offer value (10) apply scarcity/urgency to the bonuses themselves ("never available anywhere else", "only for buyers this week").
- Advanced: get non-competing businesses to contribute products/services as free bonuses in exchange for exposure to your customers — then negotiate referral commissions on top, turning bonuses into a profit stream ($400 offer generating $350 extra pure profit).
- What's core vs bonus? The "wow factor": distinct items that can stand alone, especially short-but-high-value ones (checklists, maps, swipe files).
- Build a vault of reusable assets: every workshop recording, template, checklist, interview — sprinkle into offers to close deals at zero marginal cost.

## Anti-patterns
- Discounting instead of adding value.
- Unnamed, unpriced bonuses ("includes support") — invisible = worthless.
- Trainings where tools would do (higher effort = lower perceived value).

## Worked Example
Infomercial economics: $38.95 knife becomes irresistible after 37 knives + sharpeners + pans + guarantees are stacked with escalating value tags — established price, then expansion until saying no feels stupid.

## Key Takeaways
- Local Local service bonus stack example: "Patient-Review Engine" templates ( value), "Google Maps Ranking Checklist" (), WhatsApp booking setup (), 30-day tweak window (USD3,000) — all zero marginal cost, all objection-killers.
- Record everything we make once; bank it in a bonus vault.
- Partner bonuses: print shops (local service businesses' lookbooks), local service suppliers, local photographers — free client perks + referral commissions.
