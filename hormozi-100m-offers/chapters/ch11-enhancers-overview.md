# Chapter 11: Enhancing The Offer — Scarcity, Urgency, Bonuses, Guarantees, Naming (Overview)

## Core Idea
After the core offer is built, five outside levers shift the demand curve without changing the product: scarcity (↓supply), urgency (↑demand via deadlines), bonuses (↑value perception), guarantees (reverse risk), naming (re-stimulate demand). The Arnold fundraiser raised $5.4M from 100 people by cutting supply, raising price, and auctioning one-of-a-kind items.

## Frameworks Introduced
- Delicate Dance of Desire
- Hormozi Law: the longer you delay the ask/satisfaction, the bigger the ask you can make

## Key Concepts
- Perfect profit combination: lots of demand, little perceived supply.
- Desire comes from NOT having: "Desire is a contract you make with yourself to be unhappy until you get what you want" (Naval). Satisfying all demand kills the goose; satisfying none starves you.
- Demand is fractal (80/20): sell 2 seats at $5,000 rather than 10 at $500 — more profit, less cost, pent-up desire remains in the other 8, making next launch hotter.
- Pulling the trigger too early means each successive promotion sells fewer units until demand is exhausted.
- Levers map: scarcity→exclusivity; urgency→action threshold; bonuses→price-to-value gap; guarantees→risk; naming→awareness/response rate.

## Anti-patterns
- Selling everything, always, to everyone at full availability (max short-term revenue, dead future demand).
- Fake scarcity/deadlines that reset visibly — destroys credibility permanently.

## Worked Example
Charity gala: cut tickets 15k→25k price with fewer seats; demand rose; auctions framed as never-repeating one-offs with bonuses thrown in during bidding wars. $54,000 per head average.

## Key Takeaways
- For Local Agency: cap monthly sprints below capacity, run dated promos, stack named bonuses, guarantee the outcome, rename per season.
- Track "pent-up demand": people who inquired and didn't buy are next month's hottest leads if the offer stays scarce.
