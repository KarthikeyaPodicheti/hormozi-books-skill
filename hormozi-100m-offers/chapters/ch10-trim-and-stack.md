# Chapter 10: Creating Your Grand Slam Offer Part II — Trim & Stack

## Core Idea
Steps 4-5: brainstorm every delivery vehicle for each solution (divergent), then trim high-cost/low-value and low-cost/low-value items, keeping high-value items at lowest cost, and stack them into ONE named bundle with per-item price tags.

## Frameworks Introduced
- Sales-to-Fulfillment Continuum
- Delivery Vehicle Cheat Codes (the "delivery cube")
- Trim & Stack
- 10x–1/10th test

## Key Concepts
- Continuum: doing more makes selling easier but fulfillment harder. Start by over-delivering insanely ("create flow, monetize flow, then add friction"), then systemize to reclaim margin — Gym Launch went from flying out to gyms (done-for-you) to teaching the same promise (done-with-you) at scale.
- Cheat codes for generating vehicles: attention level (1-on-1 / small group / one-to-many); effort mode (DIY/DWY/DFY); medium (in-person, phone, text, email, Zoom); consumption format (audio/video/written); response speed (24/7 vs business hours vs 5 minutes); plus the 10x test ("at 10x price what would I include?") and 1/10th test ("how would I still deliver value cheaper?").
- One-to-many assets are the margin goldmine: high one-time creation cost, ~zero marginal cost (his Excel meal-plan generator: 100 hours once, then "personalized plans" in 15 min). This is why software wins.
- Trim order: cut high-cost/low-value first, then low-cost/low-value. Keep high-value/low-cost AND justified high-value/high-cost.
- The final stack gets benefit-driven names + dollar values per component (a small ad budget Foolproof Bargain Grocery System + a prior payment amount Busy Parent Cooking Guide + ... = "$4,351 total value, all for $599").
- A stacked bundle does three jobs: solves ALL perceived problems, gives you conviction yours is one-of-a-kind, makes comparison impossible.

## Anti-patterns
- Presenting everything as one undifferentiated lump ("a website") — invisible value.
- Keeping romantic high-cost deliverables that don't move the Value Equation.
- Building the perfect efficient machine before any cash flow exists.

## Worked Example
The weight-loss bundle: grocery system, cooking guide, personalized meal plan, fat-burning workouts, travel blueprint, accountability system, eating-out system — each named, each valued — total $4,351 sold at $599 (later re-sold by his gyms at $2,400-$5,200 as their own value-stacking matured).

## Key Takeaways
- Local Agency stack sketch: core sprint + done-for-you copywriting + photo guidance checklist + Google Business Profile setup + WhatsApp booking button + 30-day tweaks + review-request templates — each NAMED and VALUED against the your base price anchor.
- Build reusable one-to-many assets (template libraries, niche content packs) once; deploy into every future client's stack.
- Never present "website design" as a line item again — always as a named bundle.
