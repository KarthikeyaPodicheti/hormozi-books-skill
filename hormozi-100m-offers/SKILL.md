# Hormozi $100M Offers — Applied to Local Agencies

Source: Alex Hormozi, *$100M Offers* (Acquisition.com Vol. I). Use when building, pricing, or enhancing any Local Agency offer (website sprints, retainers, upsells), writing sales pages/cold DMs for local service businesses or local service businesses, or answering pricing objections.

## Core Frameworks

**Grand Slam Offer**: an offer so good people feel stupid saying no. An offer that cannot be compared to anything else — attractive promotion + unmatchable value proposition + premium price + unbeatable guarantee + payment terms (money model) that let you get paid to acquire customers. It converts a price-driven purchase into a value-driven one: you sell in a "category of one," so the prospect compares your offer to nothing. Effects multiply: ↑response rate × ↑close rate × ↑price = e.g. 2.5×2.5×4 ≈ 22x more cash collected on the same ad spend. Same fulfillment work either way — the offer changes what it's worth.

**Value Equation**: `Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement) / (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice)`. Increase the top two, decrease the bottom two. Beginners fight over Dream Outcome (bigger claims); world-class companies attack the bottom half — make it faster and effortless (Apple, Amazon, Netflix). If Time Delay → 0 and Effort → 0, value approaches infinity (instant results, zero work = why DFY > DIY). Perception IS reality: a dotted subway map cut perceived wait more than billion-dollar faster trains. For Local Agency the sprint format is itself bottom-half engineering: website live in days not months (Time ↓), client writes nothing / touches no tech (Effort ↓).

**Premium Pricing / Virtuous Cycle of Price**: charge as much as you can say without cracking a smile — but raise price only after raising value, so the buyer still gets a deal (buy USD1,00,000 of value for USD10,000 = "money at a discount"). Raising price literally raises perceived value (same wine rated better at higher price) and increases client investment → adherence → results. Lowering price does the reverse: less invested clients, worse clients, margins that can't fund quality — the race to the bottom. Never compete on price; there's no strategic benefit to being second cheapest. Niching multiplies price up to 100x for identical work: generic time management course $19 vs same content for B2B power-tool sales reps $2,000. A "website" is a commodity; a "patient-booking website sprint built only for local service businesses" is a category of one. Note: Hormozi says charge what it's WORTH (value delivered), which licenses premium pricing even from a low base price like your base price — anchor against outcome value (new patients booked), never against other freelancers' rates.

**Starving Crowd (Market Selection)**: Market > Offer > Persuasion skills in order of importance. Four indicators: Massive Pain, Purchasing Power, Easy to Target, Growing. Health/Wealth/Relationships are evergreen demand pools. Don't be romantic about your audience; commit to one niche ("don't make me niche slap you") — under $10M/year revenue, niching always makes more money. Dentists and local service businesses both pass: pain (no bookings / no project inquiries), money (high per-customer lifetime value), easy targeting (associations, local FB groups, Instagram hashtags), growing markets.

**The 5-Step Offer Creation Process**: (1) Identify the Dream Outcome — sell the destination, not the vehicle (not "a gym membership," but "lose 20 lbs in 6 weeks"; for us: not "a website," but "a booking-ready site in 7 days"). (2) List every problem the prospect will hit before/during/after — walk their journey in insane detail (4 problem buckets mirror the Value Equation: worth-it? will-it-work-for-me? too-hard? too-long?). More problems = more things to solve = more value. (3) Convert each problem into a solution statement ("How to ___ so that ___"). Solve EVERY one — a single unsolved objection kills sales. (4) Brainstorm delivery vehicles for each solution using the cheat codes: 1-on-1 / small group / 1-to-many; DIY/DWY/DFY; medium (in-person, call, text, email); response speed; and the 10x–1/10th test ("what would I give at 10x the price? how would I still deliver at 1/10th?"). (5) Trim & Stack: cut high-cost/low-value items first, keep high-value/low-cost, then bundle survivors into named components with dollar values attached.

**Stacking / Bonus Presentation**: an offer broken into named parts presented as bonuses beats the same offer as one lump. Anchor the core offer price, then stack bonuses with names, justifications, and price tags until total stated value dwarfs the price ($4,351 of value, all for $599). Bonus rules: benefit-driven name; explain what/how-discovered/what-life-looks-like-after; add proof; tools & checklists > trainings (lower effort = higher value); bonus value should exceed core offer value; apply scarcity/urgency TO the bonuses themselves. Advanced: get other businesses' products as free bonuses for exposure, plus negotiated referral commissions. Never discount the core offer to close — add a matched bonus instead.

**Scarcity (quantity)**: people want what they can't have; fear of loss beats desire for gain. Three types: limited seats/slots, limited bonuses, never-available-again. Honest scarcity is easiest and ethical — publish your real capacity ("we take 4 sprint slots per week; 3 are booked") and report % to capacity. Always sell out rather than over-supply; announce the sell-out (it's social proof + stored desire). Service variants: Total Business Cap, Growth Rate Cap (X new clients/week), Cohort Cap (X per start date).

**Urgency (time)**: scarcity limits how many; urgency limits when. Four ethical forms: (1) Rolling cohort deadlines ("sign today to be in Monday's kickoff"), (2) Rolling seasonal urgency (re-wrap the same promo with real dated seasons), (3) Pricing/bonus-based urgency (the discount or bonus expires, not the service), (4) Exploding opportunity (the arbitrage genuinely decays). Deadlines drive decisions — 50-60% of launch sales happen in the last 4 hours. Announce price rises in advance ("price goes up Friday") to flush the pipeline.

**Guarantees (risk reversal)**: risk is the #1 objection; reversing it can 2-4x conversion. Guarantee math: doubling refunds 5%→10% while closing 130% still nets +23% profit — don't be emotional, do the math. Four types: **Unconditional** (no-questions refund — strongest, best low-ticket/B2C), **Conditional** (if-you-do-X-and-don't-get-Y-then-Z; creative versions: service guarantee [work free until result], double/triple-back, delayed second payment [don't bill until first outcome], credit-based, release-of-contract), **Anti-Guarantee** ("all sales final" + a bold reason why — filters out tire-kickers), **Implied** (performance/revshare/pay-per-result — most aligned, needs trackable outcomes). Stack guarantees too (unconditional short-window + conditional long-window). Tie conditions to actions the client must take anyway — guarantee-hunters make bad customers. Name guarantees memorably.

**Naming (M-A-G-I-C formula)**: **M**agnetic reason-why (Free / 88% off / Anniversary / New Year — can be almost anything), **A**vatar (call out exactly who it's for, hyper-local if possible), **G**oal (the dream outcome in words), **I**nterval (timeframe — "21-Day", "6-Week"), **C**ontainer word (Challenge, Blueprint, Sprint, Accelerator, Bootcamp — signals a bundle, not a commodity). Use 3-5 components, punchy, rhyme/alliteration nice-to-have. When offers fatigue, change wrapping paper in this order: creative → copy → headline/name → duration → enhancer → monetization structure — never touch a converting offer's guts.

**Sales-to-Fulfillment Continuum & Hormozi Laws**: doing more makes selling easier but fulfilling harder. Over-deliver insanely at first to create cash flow, then systemize the delivery to reclaim margin. "Create flow, monetize flow, then add friction." Hormozi Law: the longer you delay satisfaction of desire (keep supply under demand), the bigger the ask you can make.
