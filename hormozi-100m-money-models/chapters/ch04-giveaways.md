# Chapter 4 — Giveaways

## Core Idea
Advertise a chance to win a Grand Prize in exchange for contact info and qualifying actions. One person wins the prize; EVERYONE else "qualifies" for a discounted promotional offer on the very thing they showed interest in. Many will enter... many will buy.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Giveaway Offer** mechanics: Grand Prize → promotional offer → entry/eligibility → deadline → winner announcement → discount calls

## Key Concepts
- Grand Prize = the thing you want everyone to buy, anchored at full value (" premium business site").
- Promotional discount rule of thumb: 10–30% of gross margin; explain cost-to-value gap ($5,000 value / $1,800 price reads as 64% off even at 10% real discount).
- Urgency in three places: to ENTER (3–7 days), to CLAIM (7 days), to USE (hours–5 days).
- Two prizes double leads: "if someone you refer wins, you win too."
- If the giveaway flops, the prize wasn't grand enough.
- Eligibility questions ("why should we pick you?") harvest sales ammo for the calls.
- Recurring businesses: apply the discount across the longest term they'll accept.

## Anti-patterns
- Giving away something cheap or internal (event tickets) — nobody cares.
- Fake giveaways with no real winner (illegal + reputation poison).
- No deadline anywhere in the funnel.

## Worked Example
Fitness certification company: advertised one full-ride scholarship; applicants submitted stories; one won, everyone else got a "partial scholarship" call and most enrolled on the spot — without ever knowing the list price first.

**Example play:** "Win a FREE website makeover for your local service business (worth )." Entrants: local service businesses/local service businesses answering "what's costing you patients/clients?" Winner gets the premium build. Everyone else gets off the sprint + free GBP setup, expiring in 7 days.

## Key Takeaways
- Giveaways generate qualified leads pre-sold on your most expensive thing.
- Everyone who entered wants the prize — so everyone gets an offer.
