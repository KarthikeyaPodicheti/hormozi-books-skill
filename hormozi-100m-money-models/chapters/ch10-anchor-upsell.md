# Chapter 10 — Anchor Upsell

## Core Idea
Show the genuinely expensive option first. Expect **The Gasp**. Then rescue with your main offer — which now feels like a bargain — keeping core functions identical and swapping only "premium" secondary features. Anchored buyers spend more than planned, and some buy the anchor.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Anchor Upsell 5 steps:** (1) present anchor (2) get The Gasp (3) come to the rescue — ask if they care about the premium differentiator (4) present main offer (5) ask how they want to pay

## Key Concepts
- Anchor should be 5–10x the main offer's price.
- Same PRIMARY features in both (a suit is a suit); differ only on secondary ones (designer, material).
- The anchor must be REAL and sincerely pitched — fake anchors read as fake and destroy trust.
- Bigger gasp correlates with more buying; if nobody gasps, some will just buy the anchor — take the money.
- Make an anchor you'd be happy to deliver.

## Anti-patterns
- Treating the anchor as a joke ("just showing options") — customer treats it as one too.
- Inventing a premium tier you'd hate to fulfill.
- Apologizing for the anchor price.

## Worked Example
Suit shop: Hormozi budgets $500. Owner presents a $16,000 suit first (gasp), asks "do you care about the designer?", produces the $2,200 suit already pulled off the rack, adds socks/shirt/handkerchief ($300). Total spend: $2,500 — 5x budget — feeling like a smart buyer.

## Key Takeaways
- Order of presentation changes perceived value without changing the product.
- Premium tiers also capture the whales who never gasp.

**Example play:** pitch "Practice Growth System" — brand identity + premium site + booking + monthly marketing retainer at /year first. At the gasp: "Do you care about custom branding, or getting patients this month?" → Launch Sprint feels cheap. Some established local service businesses will take the USD60k.
