# Chapter 2 — The Four Types of Offers That Make Money Models

## Core Idea
All Money Models combine four offer types in sequence: **Attraction** (strangers→customers), **Upsell** (spend more), **Downsell** (say yes when they'd say no), **Continuity** (keep buying). Each fixes what the previous one leaks.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Four Offer Types** taxonomy
- **Three-stage maturity:** Stage I Get Cash (Attraction) → Stage II Get More Cash (Upsell+Downsell) → Stage III Get The Most Cash (Continuity)

## Key Concepts
- No attraction offer = no customers. Only one product = money left on table. No downsell = nos stay nos. No continuity = revenue resets to zero monthly.
- Any offer can be used standalone, at any stage, in any order — the sequence is the default, not a law.
- Ground rules: refund freely; hard-selling is for weak products; be transparent; check legality of "free"; ask "how will I make this work for MY business?"

## Anti-patterns
- Implementing all four types simultaneously before any works reliably.
- Treating "this won't work for my industry" as an answer instead of a design prompt.
- Convincing unwilling people instead of finding people who want it.

## Worked Example
Hormozi's gym stack: attraction = Free 6-Week Challenge ($39 lead-in); upsell = supplements (+$80/customer); back-end = "a prior payment amount challenge fee credited toward annual membership" (rollover into continuity). $1 in ads returned $34 in 48 hours — which is why a mentor told him he was running a level-10 skill in a level-2 opportunity.

**Example play:** your four slots today — Attraction: decoy/free-audit offer; Upsell: Launch Package add-ons; Downsell: payment plan on the sprint; Continuity: care plan. Build them in that order, one working before the next.

## Key Takeaways
- Four offer types × right timing = a Money Model.
- Stage-gate yourself: don't add Stage III before Stage I prints cash.
