# Chapter 12 — Payment Plan Downsells

## Core Idea
"It costs too much" almost always means "too much RIGHT NOW." Keep price and product identical; change WHEN they pay. A payment plan converts like a discount but pays like full price. It's a gamble — this chapter stacks the odds.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Payment Plan Downsell, 7 steps** (stop when they buy):
1. Quote price WITH interest baked in; reward paid-in-full as a discount ("... or _PLUS if you prepay — most people do")
2. Third-party financing / credit card ("want me deciding your payment terms, or you?") / layaway
3. Half now, half on payday ("when do you get paid?" / "what CAN you put down today?")
4. Temperature check: "1–10, how badly do you want this?" ≥8 continue; ≤7 ask "why not a 10?" → feature downsell instead
5. Three payments
6. Evenly spread across the service period
7. Free Trial (ch13)

## Key Concepts
- **Seesaw downsell** (simpler): "giant monthly payments or tiny ones?" → tiny → prepay kills them → adjust DOWN PAYMENT until monthly fits.
- Schedule charges on PAYDAYS; re-run declined cards same day (recovers ~⅓).
- Built-in upsell: periodic offer to pay the balance today and still get the original prepay discount.
- Health metric: total closes UP while % paying-in-full stays FLAT. If PIF% drops, you moved would-be cash payers onto plans — worst outcome.
- Churn by billing cadence (ProfitWell, 14k businesses): monthly 10.7%, quarterly 5%, annual 2%. Fewer bigger payments = stickier clients.
- Incentivize faster payment and they'll pay faster.

## Anti-patterns
- Discounting price just to close (customers talk; it's not a downsell, it's capitulation).
- Monthly-only plans when quarterly/annual terms are possible.

## Worked Example
Hormozi month one, rent due: prospect says "can't afford it." He improvises: half now/half later → no → three payments → no → "what can you do?" → "the whole thing on the 1st" (payday). Charged card on the 2nd. First-ever payment plan worked — later systematized up to nine offers per sale.

## Key Takeaways
- Nine offers per sale is normal at mastery; stop at yes.
- The plan protects full price; the discount rewards speed.

**Example play:** sprint price objection → " a portion today, the rest on your payday when your service revenue lands" → else three weekly payments → else temperature check → feature downsell (ch14).
