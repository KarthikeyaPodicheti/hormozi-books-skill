# Chapter 6 — Buy X Get Y Free

## Core Idea
Reframe price as free stuff. Selling 3 items at fair price is boring; selling 1 at 3x with 2 FREE is an event — same economics, way more pull. Free beats discount in attention every time.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Buy X Get Y Free**: paid item(s) + more free item(s); works on quantity OR time ("buy 6 months get 12 free")

## Key Concepts
- Give MORE free than paid (Buy 2 Get 10 > Buy 10 Get 2).
- Free things can differ from paid things (buy shirt, get 3 socks); sometimes many cheap frees beat few expensive frees.
- RAISE PRICES FIRST, then give value away — don't lie about value.
- Prepaid customers keep buying — keep making offers to them (wallets "refresh").
- One-time-purchase audiences? Make that single purchase as big as possible (Boot Factory tourists).
- Cash pop from existing recurring clients: offer prepaid bulk to ≤10% of them.
- If you collect a year of cash in a month, budget it for a year of delivery.

## Anti-patterns
- Giving away your only product without repricing (you go hungry).
- Buy-one-get-one symmetry instead of generous free ratios.
- Spending prepaid delivery money on anything else.

## Worked Example
Boot Factory Nashville: "BUY 1 PAIR GET TWO PAIR FREE" — one pair priced at a prior payment amount (~3x normal). Tourists in cowboy bars bought matching boots in groups. Store thrived while neighbors died. Service version: "18 months for $1,800" framed three ways — Good: buy 12 get 6; Better: buy 9 get 9; **Best: buy 6 get 12 free** — same revenue, most compelling frame wins.

## Key Takeaways
- Same money, better story: convert discounts into free units/time.
- Best used to pull cash forward (prepaid terms) — exactly what cash-hungry agencies need.

**Example play:** " sprint + 6 months of care plan FREE" instead of "sprint your base price + care ×6." Collect upfront, deliver care over time, client feels like they robbed you.
