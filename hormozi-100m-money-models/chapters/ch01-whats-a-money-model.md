# Chapter 1 — What's A Money Model?

## Core Idea
A Money Model is a **sequence of offers** — what you offer, when, and how — designed to get more customers, make each worth more, and collect the cash faster. Bad Money Models kill businesses: they spend more acquiring a customer than the customer returns in time, forcing ad cuts, loans, and death. Good ones make getting customers free — or profitable.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Money Model** (definition + rental-car walkthrough: a low daily rate car became $100/day through 5 well-timed offers)
- **Acquisition / Monetization / Recoupment** framing: grow all three and business growth multiplies (2×2×2 = 8x)
- **The 30-day standard**: profit from one customer should cover cost of getting + servicing at least two more within 30 days

## Key Concepts
- Every offer solves a problem the customer just realized they have; solve it at the moment of realization.
- "It costs too much" usually means "it costs too much RIGHT NOW" — speed of payment is the lever, not price.
- Death spiral: overspend on CAC → cut ads → starve → borrow → sell equity → close. The fix is getting paid fast, not waiting years for LTV.

## Anti-patterns
- One offer only ("you barely have a business — you have a front end").
- Waiting months/years for payback while cash-starved.
- Copying another business's model instead of designing your own sequence.

## Worked Example
Rental car counter: reserved a low daily rate economy. Upsell #1 roomier truck; #2 late-return flexibility; #3 premium insurance; #4 minimum-insurance downsell; #5 prepaid gas (a small price difference). Customer happily pays 5x because each offer solved a problem exactly when it appeared. Industry-wide, this sequence prints billions monthly.

**Example play:** map your own client journey — after "yes" to the your base price sprint, what problems appear immediately? Domain? Google listing? Photos? Booking? Each is an offer slot with a moment attached.

## Key Takeaways
- More offers > one offer; sequenced offers > random offers.
- Speed of collection beats size of eventual profit when cash-constrained.
- Design the sequence around problem-realization moments.
