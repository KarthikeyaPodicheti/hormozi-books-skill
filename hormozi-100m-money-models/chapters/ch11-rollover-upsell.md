# Chapter 11 — Rollover Upsell

## Core Idea
Credit some or all of what a client already paid toward your NEXT, more expensive offer. "You've already spent your base price — roll it into the annual plan." Converts refunds into revenue, churn into upgrades, and old clients into fresh sales.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Rollover Upsell**: decide WHO (old clients, upset clients, competitors' upset clients, current clients), WHAT (more/better/new), HOW (full or partial credit, upfront or spread over term)

## Key Concepts
- **Pricing rule:** price the upsell ≥4x the credit → max 25% effective discount, profit survives.
- **Spread credit over the term** ( paid → USD500/month off a 12-month plan) so they start paying NOW instead of riding free months.
- Winback campaigns: personalized videos to past clients offering credit — 20% take rate produced ~$1.9M ARR for Hormozi.
- Before any refund: "wanna just roll that into X instead?"
- Steal competitors' unhappy customers: credit what they still owe the competitor toward your agreement (find them via negative reviews).
- Urgency: make it one-time, right-now, once-in-a-customer-lifetime.

## Anti-patterns
- Crediting full amounts onto things cheaper than the credit.
- Giving winners free months upfront (kills paying momentum).
- Refunding first and asking questions never.

## Worked Example
Hormozi's gym: challenge winners took 3 free months, then quit before ever paying. Justin's gym rolled winnings INTO a year membership ($50/month off × 12) — winners started paying immediately. That single mechanic fixed his entire recurring-revenue problem and became core to Gym Launch.

## Key Takeaways
- Previous payments are negotiating chips, not sunk costs.
- Roll toward MORE expensive offers to keep margin.

**Example play:** local service business unhappy with template site? Don't refund: "roll your your base investment into the custom conversion site at a premium custom price." Old client from 2024? "Your your base investment is back as credit toward a 12-month Growth Care plan." Competitor's abandoned-site victim on Google reviews? Credit their loss toward your rebuild.
