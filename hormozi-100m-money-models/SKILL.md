# Hormozi $100M Money Models — Applied to Local Agencies

Source: Alex Hormozi, *$100M Money Models* (2025). A **Money Model** is a deliberate sequence of offers — what you offer, when, and how — so you make more money per customer, faster, than it costs to acquire them. Goal standard: profit from ONE client covers getting and servicing TWO more within 30 days.

**Local Agency/service business context:** low-ticket (your base price) website sprints sold to local service businesses. The binding constraint is cash flow, not margin-per-unit. So this skill weights everything toward: cash upfront > payment plans > upsell ladders > continuity. Every framework below ends with an "Example play."

## Core Frameworks

### The Three Jobs of a Money Model
1. **Acquisition** — how many customers you get (Attraction Offers)
2. **Monetization** — how much each pays (Upsell + Downsell Offers)
3. **Recoupment** — how fast you get your CAC back (sequencing + upfront cash + Continuity)

Growth compounds multiplicatively: double customers × double value × half payback time = 8x faster growth.

### The Four Offer Types (the sequence)
1. **Attraction Offers** turn strangers into customers — free/discounted entry that still collects cash.
2. **Upsell Offers** = whatever you offer NEXT. Each purchase reveals a new problem; sell its solution immediately.
3. **Downsell Offers** = whatever you offer after a NO. Change *how they pay* (payment plan, trial-with-penalty) or *what they get* (feature downsell) — never just cut price.
4. **Continuity Offers** keep them paying monthly (bonuses, discounts, waived fees).

Three stages of maturity — build one at a time:
- **Stage I: Get Cash** (Attraction gets customers who pay for themselves)
- **Stage II: Get More Cash** (upsells/downsells push 30-day profit past CAC+delivery)
- **Stage III: Get The Most Cash** (continuity stacks recurring revenue)

**Example play:** Stage I = Decoy sprint offer (see ch04). Stage II = Classic upsell ladder: website → Google Business Profile setup → booking integration → monthly care plan. Stage III = /month hosting + updates + content edits ("website never goes stale").

### Attraction Offers (get customers AND collect cash)
- **Win Your Money Back:** client deposits full price; refundable as cash/store credit if they hit tracked goals/actions (e.g., "your site live in 7 days + you post it + 2 referral intros = money back as credit toward SEO"). ~10% will claim refunds — only run if refund rate <5%. Apply won credit SPREAD over a longer commitment (Rollover), not as free months upfront.
- **Giveaways:** advertise a Grand Prize (e.g., free premium site worth ); everyone else "qualifies" for a partial scholarship/discount on your core offer. Discount = 10–30% of gross margin. Deadlines to enter, to claim, to use.
- **Decoy Offer:** advertise the cheap/simple version; when they engage, present it beside a premium version with guarantees and support. Ask "free stuff or real results?" Expect most to take premium. Discounted offers show up more than free ones.
- **Buy X Get Y Free:** reframe price into free stuff — " for the sprint + 3 months of care FREE" beats "20% off." Give MORE free than paid (Buy 6 Get 12 > Buy 12 Get 6). Raise base prices before giving things away.
- **Pay Less Now or Pay More Later:** "$0 now, billed in 7 days unless cancelled" vs "pay the full price today + bonuses." Card on file either way. If >10% cancel billing, you over-promised or under-conditioned.

**Example play:** default attraction = Decoy ("free DIY checklist" vs "done-for-you sprint"); seasonal = Giveaway ("win a free business site; everyone else gets a discount off").

### Upsell Offers (whatever comes next)
- **Classic Upsell:** "You can't have X without Y." Website without a Google Business Profile is invisible; a local business site without appointment booking leaks patients. Offer the moment the need becomes obvious. Charge for guarantees/warranties instead of giving them free (+5–50%).
- **Menu Upsell:** Unsell (cross out what they don't need) → Prescribe (exactly what they do need, personalized) → A/B choice (which flavor: "logo refresh A or B?") → Card on file ("use the same card?"). Never ask yes/no buy questions; ask preference questions.
- **Anchor Upsell:** show the premium first ( brand + site + marketing retainer); expect "The Gasp"; rescue with the main offer ( sprint) which now feels cheap. Anchor must be real and same core function. Some WILL buy the anchor.
- **Rollover Upsell:** credit previous payments toward something MORE expensive (price it ≥4x the credit so max discount = 25%). Uses: winbacks (old clients), upset clients (instead of refund), competitors' unhappy clients, current clients buying bigger packages. Spread credit over the term (a prior payment amount → /month off × 12) so they keep paying NOW.

**Sample upsell ladder (example):**
1. Sprint site at your base price → 2. GBP + maps optimization → 3. Booking form + WhatsApp widget → 4. Photo/brand polish → 5. Care plan /month (continuity). Bundle steps 2–4 as "Launch Package a package price" — named bundles outsell itemized lists.

### Downsell Offers (when they say no)
Rules: no ≠ never; downsells are trades; personalize don't pressure; NEVER drop price for the same thing (customers talk).
- **Payment Plan Downsell (7 steps, stop when they buy):** (1) quote price WITH interest built in, reward paid-in-full with discount (", or _PLUS if you prepay") (2) third-party financing / credit card / layaway (3) half now, half on payday (4) temperature check: 1–10, how badly do you want this? ≥8 keep going, ≤7 feature-downsell (5) three payments (6) evenly spread across delivery milestones (7) Trial With Penalty. Schedule charges on PAYDAYS. Seesaw variant: "giant or tiny monthly payments?" → prepay discount → adjust down payment until monthly fits.
- **Trial With Penalty:** free start IF they meet terms (attend kickoff call, send content by day 3, approve designs in 48h); miss = fee. Always take card + ID. Sell "staying long-term" BEFORE trial starts. Break fees small per miss rather than one lump fee. Call it a "trial," not a penalty.
- **Feature Downsell:** lower price BY REMOVING things (never discount same thing): fewer pages, template not custom, email-only revisions, DIY content upload, no guarantee, DFY→DWY→DIY. Removing the GUARANTEE often flips people back to full price. Name tiers ("Complete / Standard / Minimum"). Alternate payment-plan ↔ feature downsells; ask "Deal?" or "Fair enough?" after each.

**Example play:** local service business says the price is too much → " a portion today, the rest on your payday" → else 3-page starter (drop booking + gallery) → else "you send me text/photos, I assemble" (DIY-lite at ). Never just say "okay USD3,000."

### Continuity Offers (keep them paying)
- **Continuity Bonus:** join the care plan today, get X free (bonus value > first month's payment). Advertise the bonus, not the membership.
- **Continuity Discount:** commit to a term, get free time — apply UP FRONT (more signups, more churn), AT THE END (earned free months, low churn), or SPREAD (/month off × 12). Bill every 4 WEEKS not monthly = +8.3% revenue/year free. Add 3% processing fee; offer to waive it for a second payment method on file.
- **Waived Fee:** month-to-month costs setup; commit 12 months and it's waived; quit early = pay it. Fee = 3–5x monthly rate; minimum 12-month commitments. If leaving costs more than staying, they stay.
- **Standalone-vs-membership pricing:** price the one-time option 1.33x for 50% choosing membership, 2x for 70%, 2.66x for 90%.
- Cancellation policy: fee = the discount they received. Make cancelling easy to find; run exit interviews (waive fee for feedback) — saves ~⅓.

### Client Financed Acquisition
The master frame: structure the sequence so the CLIENT'S cash (upfront payments, prepaid terms, upsells inside 30 days) pays for acquiring the next client — you grow ads-led growth without debt or investors. Test: does one client's 30-day gross profit cover CAC + delivery of ≥2 more?

## Hard Rules (from Hormozi)
Refund anyone who asks. Don't hard-sell weak products. Never lie about values/prices. Obey local law on "free" offers. One offer at a time until reliable. Simple scales, fancy fails — 100 ways to offer one product beats 100 products.
