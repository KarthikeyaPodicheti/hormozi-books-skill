# Chapter 14 — #3 Agencies

## Core Idea
Don't rent results forever — use agencies to buy skills you keep. Standard agency relationships decay (senior rep replaced by junior, results sag, cancel, repeat). The alternative: hire with an explicit learning contract and a deadline to beat them.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The agency learning contract**: "I want to learn what you do; 6 months together; I'll pay extra for decision transparency; then my team takes over and we downgrade to consulting." Most agencies accept; negotiate price otherwise.
- **Two-agency strategy**: hire a cheap "good enough" agency to learn platform basics + an elite one (even 4x price) to learn maximization; drop to consulting when your team outperforms.
- **Good-agency checklist (10 signs)**: word-of-mouth clients you know; recognizable results; waiting list; realistic-expectation sales process; long-term strategy talk (no hacks); clear input requirements; scheduled updates; plain-language cost-vs-result tracking; a real Value-Equation offer; expensive (all good ones are — not all expensive ones are good).
- Benchmark: get your team to agency-level in <6 months (Hormozi compressed a year → 10 → 8 → <6 months).

## Key Concepts
- You get only a fraction of an agency's attention, especially after their next big client signs; your team improves full-time on you.
- Use agencies specifically for (a) new methods, (b) new platforms — skipping trial-and-error tuition.
- Double-pay period (agency + your team learning simultaneously) is the planned cost of ownership.

## Anti-patterns
- The 11-step doom loop: excitement → senior rep → junior rep → complaints → cancel → repeat.
- Believing "I'll never need to learn this."
- Choosing the cheapest agency (no money = don't hire agencies at all; learn by doing).

## Worked Example
Young Hormozi couldn't afford a retainer, so he negotiated $750/hour teaching sessions: two calls watched, two driving, clicked by call five, self-sufficient by eight — $6,000 total for a skill that made millions. Every later agency failure taught him that first instinct (buy the skill, not the service) was right.

## Key Takeaways
- For Local Agency, agencies are mostly premature — but the pattern applies to ANY expert (freelance editor, ads freelancer): always contract for knowledge transfer, never pure delegation.
- If ever hiring an ads freelancer: demand screen-shares of decisions, timeline to internalize, downgrade clause.
- Compare their numbers vs yours monthly; cancel when you win twice.
