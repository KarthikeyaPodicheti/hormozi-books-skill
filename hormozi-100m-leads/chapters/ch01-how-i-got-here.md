# Chapter 1 — How I Got Here

## Core Idea
Knowing how to get leads saved Hormozi's business repeatedly; it's the one skill that generates second chances. Leads are upstream of everything: offers, sales, and pricing only matter if strangers find out you exist.

## Frameworks Introduced
- The lead machine (informal): Gym Launch was built on warm referrals + ads + content simultaneously.
- "Getting leads is my get out of jail free card with no expiration date."

## Key Concepts
- A lead-flow crisis ($150k in refunds, zero cash) was solved in 30 days purely by switching channels and re-offering the same skills to a new audience.
- Licensing the system (selling the "how") beat doing launches manually — same knowledge, infinitely scalable delivery.
- He deliberately stacked three channels at once from day one of Gym Launch: ad leads ("I'll run ads"), content leads ("post success stories in gym groups"), referral leads ("$2,000 cash per referred gym").

## Anti-patterns
- Believing a great product markets itself — his gyms succeeded only because he aggressively advertised them.
- Scaling capacity before validating that leads flow reliably.

## Worked Example
Broke after a partner fraud, Hormozi needed USD-equivalent $150k profit in 30 days. He pivoted to selling the gym-launch system by phone: first call priced at $6,000 (a number meant to scare the buyer off — it closed), next $8,000, then $10,000. Eight yeses = $60,000 in one day, zero fulfillment cost. Then ads + gym-group posts + $2,000 referral bounties → $215k profit in 30 days → $6.8M year one.

## Key Takeaways
- When desperate, sell what you already know how to do to people who need it most.
- Stack multiple channels immediately; never depend on one.
- Price confidence is often just volume desperation inverted — say the big number.
